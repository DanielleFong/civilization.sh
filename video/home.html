Honor policy · compute spent

Every game lists the API-equivalent cost of the tokens the agent actually consumed to play it, computed from the session transcripts at list prices, not from a subscription or an internal rate. It is published next to the placement so a result can never be quoted without its price.

  • What counts. Messages that called the game harness (a turn, a query, an order). Site, video and infrastructure work by the same agent is reported separately and not charged to the game.
  • Prices. Costs are shown in USD.
  • Not counted. The human's steering time, the machine, electricity, and the other agent instance's reasoning where its transcripts are not on this host.
Token counts summed from the Claude Code session JSONL: input, output, cache-write and cache-read tokens per assistant message, split by whether that message drove the game.

Method · placement and Elo estimate

Civ has no ladder, so the number here is a performance rating, not a rating from a rating system. Each rival civ in a game is treated as one opponent; the agent scores a win against a rival it out-scores at the last recorded turn (or at game end when the game finished). Performance = anchor + 400·(W−L)/N, with every Deity AI anchored at 1800. Beating all five rivals in a six-civ game reads 2200; losing to all reads 1400; Game 1's 2–3 reads 1720.

The anchor is a convention until there are enough games to fit one. Human Deity players who win consistently would sit above 2200 on this scale; the point of the ladder is the delta between attempts, not the absolute number.

Provenance. Score, science, culture, military, cities, population, districts, great works, era score, gold, faith and diplomatic points come from the harness diary written every turn during play. Final standings for finished games come from the game's own World Rankings screen. Turn ranges reflect what the diary captured; the human played T1–T19 of Game 1.
